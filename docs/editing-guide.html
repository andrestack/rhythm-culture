How it works

Every change takes the same path

The contact, quote, booking and camp forms are wired up by me. Each enquiry is checked for spam, saved to a spreadsheet and emailed to info@rhythmculture.com.au. Hit reply and it goes straight to the customer. You don't need to edit any of that.

Which way in

Small edits in the editor. Bigger jobs by asking.

Website editor

Open it in your browser at /keystatic. It looks like a form: fields for titles, prices, text.

  • Fix a typo or update a paragraph
  • Change a price or a sale price
  • Paste in a Stripe payment link
  • Tick a product as sold out or hide it

Saved edits wait as a draft. To look at one, ask the assistant: “show me my draft.”

Claude or ChatGPT on your PC

The desktop app is pointed at your website folder and knows the house rules. You type what you want, like you'd text me.

  • Write a new blog post from your notes
  • Add photos (it shrinks them for you)
  • Add a testimonial to a page
  • Update a whole section, like term dates

A normal change

Start to finish, about five minutes

  1. Ask. “Add a post saying Term 1 drum classes start Monday 2 February in Palmwoods. Use this photo.”

  2. It makes the change on your computer. The assistant starts from the latest version of the site, writes the post, shrinks the photo and checks the site still builds.

  3. It shows you. The page opens right in the app, running only on your computer. Read it the way a customer would. Nothing is online yet. Not right? Say what to change and it shows you again.

  4. Say “save it”. It saves a draft to GitHub and tells you in plain words what it did.

  5. Say “publish”. The live site updates a couple of minutes later.

It never makes up dates, prices or payment links. If something is missing, it asks you.

Once per computer

Set up your computer, about 20 minutes

We do this together on the call. Install each one with the default options.

  1. A GitHub account. We make it on the call. I add you to the website, then you accept the invite email.

  2. Git for Windows from git-scm.com.

  3. Node 24 from nodejs.org. Pick version 24, even if a newer one is offered.

  4. GitHub CLI from cli.github.com.

  5. The Claude desktop app from claude.ai/download. Sign in.

  6. Paste the start prompt. In Claude, open the Code tab, choose your Documents folder, and paste the text below. Sign in to GitHub when a window asks.

Set up the Rhythm Culture website on this computer. Check that Git, Node 24 and the GitHub CLI are installed, and walk me through anything missing. Download https://github.com/andrestack/rhythm-culture.git into a folder called rhythm-culture here. Then follow rhythm-culture/.agents/skills/rc-setup/SKILL.md. When it's done, tell me how to open Claude on the rhythm-culture folder.

From then on, always open Claude on the rhythm-culture folder. If the preview or publishing stops working, type /rc-setup. It checks everything and says what to fix.

✦ When to call me

Most days you won't need me. These are the days you will.

Call me straight away

I fix these first
  • The live site is down, blank, or looks broken after a publish. I'll put the last version back first, then find out why.
  • A customer says a form didn't send, or enquiries have stopped arriving in the inbox.
  • A payment link charges the wrong amount, or a customer can't pay.
  • The assistant asks you to paste a password, code or token anywhere. Don't. Send me a screenshot instead.
  • The assistant says git or GitHub is “in a state”, or it can't start the site on your computer.

Ask me before you start

the assistant will say “this one needs André”
  • Changing a form: its questions, the booking terms, or where enquiries go.
  • Changing a page's web address, deleting pages or products, or changing lots of pages at once.
  • Anything new the site doesn't do yet: online bookings, an events calendar, an Instagram feed, a newsletter sign-up.
  • Design changes beyond words and photos: new layouts, colours, fonts, the menu.
  • Your domain, email, or logins for GitHub, Vercel or Stripe.
  • Legal wording: terms and conditions, privacy.

No need to call

just do it, then look on your PC
  • Typos, opening hours, class times, term dates.
  • Prices, sold out, new payment links from your Stripe account.
  • New blog posts, new photos, a new testimonial.

When you message me, send three things

  1. The page you were looking at, or its address.

  2. What you asked the assistant, copied from the chat.

  3. A screenshot of what looks wrong.
